Mexican Airstrips for your EFB and Sim

v0.38

Purpose: Find a Mexican airfield in AFAC's official list, check that it isn't already an ICAO airport, and draw its runway, ramp and taxiways from AFAC and OpenStreetMap data, and send it to ForeFlight (map layer plus a user waypoint you can put in a route) or Garmin Pilot (map layer).

1

Find the airfield

Search AFAC's list of aerodromes by designator (e.g. SBY or XSBY), OurAirports code (e.g. MX-0007), name, other names it goes by (e.g. Laguna San Ignacio) or municipality. It's the same official list that airports.robrc.net puts on a searchable map of Mexico. That site showed us the way to this data, and it's well worth a visit.

    Is it already an ICAO airport?

    An airport with an ICAO code (MMxx) is normally in ForeFlight and Garmin Pilot already, runways and all. This checks AFAC's airfield against Mexico's official airport list (Sistema Aeroportuario Mexicano) and the ICAO codes in OurAirports: same 3-letter local code, or an ICAO airport within 3 km (1.6 NM) of AFAC's point.

      4

      Runway, ramp and taxiways

      Shapes come from OpenStreetMap, checked against AFAC's figures. AFAC gives only one reference point, the runway numbers, length, width and surface, so without OpenStreetMap the runway's position and direction would be guesses.

        Enter the runway ends yourself

        Use this when OpenStreetMap has no runway, or its runway looks wrong on the satellite view. In Google Maps or Google Earth, long-press each end of the runway and copy the coordinates (decimal degrees, e.g. 26.25824, -112.50316). Coordinates entered here replace OpenStreetMap's runway.

        How this works, sources and options

        This is what the KML will draw. Zoom and pan, and switch between Satellite, Map and Topo (top right) to check the shapes against the ground before sending. Colors follow the runway surface: asphalt black, concrete gray, dirt brown, gravel tan, grass green. The ramp and taxiways are a shade lighter, and the airfield box lighter still.

        Use this when OpenStreetMap has no runway for this strip, or when its runway doesn't line up with the strip on the satellite view. You can also drag either amber end of the drawn runway. Zoom in, tap the button, then tap each end of the runway. Your line replaces OpenStreetMap's runway; any ramp and taxiways stay. To undo it, use "Back to OpenStreetMap" in step 4. If the drawn runway already sits on the strip, you don't need this.

        ForeFlight: "Open in ForeFlight" sends a ForeFlight content pack with the airfield drawn as a map layer and a user waypoint, SQA_AFAC, at AFAC's reference point with AFAC's elevation (named so it can't clash with the X-codes in other Mexican content packs). ForeFlight then only queues the pack: to finish, go to More → Custom Content, tap the pack, and tap Install. After that the airfield draws on the Maps tab, and you can type the waypoint into a flight plan like any other (to see it on the map, turn on User Waypoints in the Maps layer selector). Tap the waypoint to read the AFAC record attached to it. If tapping the button doesn't open ForeFlight, press and hold it and choose "Open in ForeFlight". Content packs need an individual ForeFlight subscription, or Performance level or higher for Business and MFB accounts. Re-installing replaces the pack and waypoint of the same name.

        Garmin Pilot: "Open in Garmin Pilot" opens the KML as a file from a web link. Tap the share button, choose "Copy to Garmin Pilot", then turn it on under Map, Overlays, KML. (Untested so far: Garmin documents KML points, lines and shapes up to 4 MB, but not colors.)

            How this works

            Builds an X-Plane 12 custom-scenery folder from the same runway, ramp and taxiways: an apt.dat (runway with AFAC's numbers, ramp and taxiway pavement, airport boundary, windsock, tie-downs, AFAC's elevation and codes) and, if you add any, a DSF with simple buildings and props from X-Plane's own library. Unzip it into X-Plane's Custom Scenery folder and fly, or open it in WorldEditor (File → Import apt.dat, then File → Import DSF) to keep working on it. Nothing here is uploaded to the Scenery Gateway. If X-Plane already has this airfield (for example from the Gateway), the two will overlap.

            Each object appears on the ramp in the map above as an amber square with its rough footprint. Drag the square to move it, and set its heading (degrees true) in the list. Footprints are approximate; X-Plane draws each model at its own size. Which way a model's door faces depends on the model, so check it in X-Plane or WorldEditor.

            Data and thanks

            • AFAC (Agencia Federal de Aviación Civil), "Aeródromos y helipuertos" list on gob.mx: edition loading…
            • ICAO check: Mexico's official airport list, "Sistema Aeroportuario Mexicano" (gob.mx, 14-Mar-2025 edition), with positions and local codes from OurAirports (public domain). Both bundled into the app 14-Nov-2026.
            • OpenStreetMap: runway, ramp, taxiway and hangar shapes, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Read through the Overpass API. If a strip is missing there, adding it to OpenStreetMap helps every pilot who uses this app.
            • Magnetic variation: NOAA/NGA World Magnetic Model WMM-2025, computed in the page with the geomagnetism library (Apache 2.0).
            • With thanks to airports.robrc.net, the public directory of Mexican airport infrastructure that showed the way to AFAC's data. Its map and its corrections to AFAC's figures are worth a look.

            Not for navigation. A planning and situational-awareness aid only. Positions come from published figures and volunteer mapping and can be wrong. Check every airfield against current official sources, recent pilot reports and your own eyes before using it.

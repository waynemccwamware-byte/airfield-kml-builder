Mexican Airstrips for your EFB and Sim

v0.97

Purpose: Find a Mexican airfield in AFAC's official list, check that it isn't already an ICAO airport, and draw its runway, ramp and taxiways from AFAC and OpenStreetMap data, and send it to ForeFlight (map layer plus a user waypoint you can put in a route) or Garmin Pilot (map layer).

Web app: plan on the ground. It needs an internet connection and keeps nothing for offline use. In flight, you have only what you've installed in ForeFlight or Garmin Pilot.

Find the airfield

Search AFAC's aerodrome list by designator (SBY or XSBY), OurAirports code (MX-0007), name, other name (Laguna San Ignacio) or municipality. It's the official list airports.robrc.net maps for all of Mexico; that site showed us the way to this data and is well worth a visit.

    Is it already an ICAO airport?

    An airport with an ICAO code (MMxx) is normally in ForeFlight and Garmin Pilot already, runways and all. This checks AFAC's airfield against Mexico's official airport list (Sistema Aeroportuario Mexicano) and the ICAO codes in OurAirports: same 3-letter local code, or an ICAO airport within 3 km (1.6 NM) of AFAC's point.

      3

      Runway, ramp and taxiways

      Shapes come from OpenStreetMap, checked against AFAC's figures. AFAC gives only one reference point, the runway numbers, length, width and surface, so without OpenStreetMap the runway's position and direction would be guesses.

        Enter the runway ends yourself

        Use this when OpenStreetMap has no runway, or its runway looks wrong on the satellite view. In Google Maps or Google Earth, long-press each end of the runway and copy the coordinates (decimal degrees, e.g. 26.25824, -112.50316). Coordinates entered here replace OpenStreetMap's runway.

        Visual approaches: advisory only

        For situational awareness only. Not an instrument approach procedure and not for navigation. The 3° path, fixes and altitudes are calculated by this app; no authority publishes or checks them. The terrain check uses a terrain model only: no towers, wires, trees or buildings, and it does not guarantee terrain or obstacle clearance. Use only in VMC with the runway in sight. You are responsible for terrain, obstacle and traffic avoidance.

        The same caution goes into every visual approach line and fix in the ForeFlight pack and KML.

        How this works, sources and options

        This is what the KML will draw. Zoom and pan, and switch between Satellite, Map and Topo (top right) to check the shapes against the ground before sending. Colors follow the runway surface: asphalt black, concrete gray, dirt brown, gravel tan, grass green. The ramp and taxiways are a shade lighter, and the airfield box lighter still.

        Use this when OpenStreetMap has no runway for this airstrip, or when its runway doesn't line up with the airstrip on the satellite view. Tap "Edit runway ends" to change them: drag either amber end, or tap the map twice to place both ends fresh, then tap Done (the ends stay locked otherwise, so panning can't move them; an Undo appears after any change). Your line replaces OpenStreetMap's runway; any ramp and taxiways stay. To undo it, use "Back to OpenStreetMap" in step 3. If the drawn runway already sits on the airstrip, you don't need this.

        ForeFlight: "Open in ForeFlight" sends a ForeFlight content pack with the airfield drawn as a map layer and a user waypoint, SQA_AFAC, midway between the runway ends (as you have them: OpenStreetMap's, or the ends you marked or dragged) with AFAC's elevation (named so it can't clash with the X-codes in other Mexican content packs). ForeFlight then only queues the pack: to finish, go to More → Custom Content, tap the pack, and tap Install. After that the airfield draws on the Maps tab, and you can type the waypoint into a flight plan like any other, with or without its layer turned on. In the Maps layer selector the pack shows as two toggles, SQA_AFAC Airfield (runway, ramp and box) and SQA_AFAC Waypoint (only needed to see and tap the waypoint on the map); ForeFlight gives every file in a pack its own toggle. Tap the waypoint to read the AFAC record attached to it. If tapping the button doesn't open ForeFlight, press and hold it and choose "Open in ForeFlight". Content packs need an individual ForeFlight subscription, or Performance level or higher for Business and MFB accounts. Sending an airstrip again (for example after adding visual approaches): ForeFlight doesn't replace a pack of the same name; it installs a second copy beside the old one, two packs with the same waypoint name (Wayne's test, 19-Nov-2026: the old SQA_AFAC pack effective 12:00am and the new one 1:49pm, both installed). So first take its waypoint out of any active flight plan, delete the old pack in ForeFlight, close and reopen ForeFlight, then send it again.

        Garmin Pilot: "Open in Garmin Pilot" opens the KML as a file from a web link. Tap the share button, choose "Copy to Garmin Pilot", then turn it on under Map, Overlays, KML. (Untested so far: Garmin documents KML points, lines and shapes up to 4 MB, but not colors.)

        Visual approaches (advisory, off to start): for each runway end, the extended centerline with a 3.00° path that crosses the threshold at 50 ft, and fixes at 2 NM, 5 NM and the outer end labeled with their MSL altitudes (amber dashes; the table lists every NM). That's how Garmin builds its own calculated visual approaches when nothing is published. Threshold elevations come from a terrain model, not AFAC's single field elevation. The terrain check always runs: from 0.5 NM out, terrain is sampled every 0.1 NM along the final and across a corridor that widens with distance (to 0.1 NM either side), against a surface 1° below the path (our margin); terrain counts only where it stands more than 50 ft above that surface, to allow for the terrain model's error. Inside 0.5 NM the runway environment is the pilot's to judge. Terrain from 3 NM out shortens the line to just inside it; terrain inside 3 NM that comes within 1° of the path keeps the line with a red warning (distance and clearance), and terrain inside 3 NM that rises above the 3° path itself means no visual approach for that end. If the terrain data doesn't load, none are drawn. The terrain model is bare earth: no towers, wires, trees or buildings. Like the pattern, it's a picture only: ForeFlight can't fly or couple it.

        Traffic patterns: dashed lines, with arrowheads on the downwinds for the direction of flight: downwind on both sides of the runway (Pattern width out, ½ NM to start), the base legs at both ends, and the finals, with a dot abeam midfield on each downwind. The base turns sit one pattern width past each runway end. Each downwind carries two arrows, one for each landing direction (for example, the north line is left downwind for 09 and right downwind for 27). AFAC's list doesn't say which side the pattern is on, so both are drawn; check local knowledge before picking one. It's a picture only: ForeFlight can't add it to a route, since a pack waypoint isn't an airport with runways.

        Offline use: this app runs on the web and needs a connection: its maps, AFAC data, runway lookups, terrain checks and pack building all come from the web. Nothing is kept for offline use. Plan on the ground, send the results to ForeFlight or Garmin Pilot, finish the install, and check them in airplane mode before you go.

        Where each piece comes from

          Start from

          Either one replaces this airfield's whole X-Plane layout (runway ends, ramp, windsock, start positions, objects, exclusion zones). Skip both to start from AFAC and OpenStreetMap.

          OpenStreetMap has no ramp at this airfield, so the app adds one (dashed outline). Drag its center square on the map to where the real ramp is on the satellite view, and its corners to resize it. Bring its edge close to the runway (or onto it) and it snaps exactly to the runway edge; the outline turns light blue when it has snapped.

            Drag the blue tie-downs (where your aircraft starts) and the orange windsock on the map to put them where you want. Each tie-down's light-blue arrow is where the aircraft's nose points at startup (facing the runway to start): drag the arrow's tip to turn it, or type the heading (°true, as X-Plane uses) in the tie-down's box. On a phone or iPad the map locks while this tab is open (lock button under the map's + / −, lit blue): one finger moves things and two fingers pinch-zoom. Tap the lock to unlock and pan, then lock again to keep arranging. Zoom in close where objects sit near each other. For more room, the expand button (under the lock) gives the map the whole window, with the Add row floating on it; Esc or the same button brings it back.

              Exclusion zones
              Zones

                An exclusion zone hides X-Plane's own scenery of the kinds you tick (trees, buildings, roads…) inside a rectangle, so it doesn't sit on your runway or ramp. Zones are always north-up rectangles (X-Plane has no rotated or polygon zones), so a slanted runway takes a row of them: "Cover the runway" lays that row along the runway and its ends (pressing it again redoes the row). Tap a zone's small pink square on the map (or its row in the list) to select it: drag the square to move it and its corners to resize it. A zone only removes scenery; it can't add trees that X-Plane doesn't draw there.

                How this works

                Builds an X-Plane 12 custom-scenery folder from the same runway, ramp and taxiways: an apt.dat (runway with AFAC's numbers, ramp and taxiway pavement, airport boundary, windsock, tie-downs, AFAC's elevation and codes) and, if you add any, a DSF with simple buildings and props from X-Plane's own library. Unzip it into X-Plane's Custom Scenery folder and fly, or open it in WorldEditor (File → Import apt.dat, then File → Import DSF) to keep working on it. Nothing here is uploaded to the Scenery Gateway. If X-Plane already has this airfield (for example from the Gateway), the two will overlap.

                "Import from Gateway" looks for an airport on the X-Plane Scenery Gateway within 5 km and brings in its recommended scenery: runway, ramp (as a rectangle), windsock, start positions and any objects this app offers (others are listed and left out). The X-Plane ID becomes the Gateway's ID, so your finished pack replaces X-Plane's own copy of that airport instead of drawing on top of it. Gateway airports are the work of their artists; credit them if you publish a change.

                Vegetation (on by default) plants X-Plane's own forests in a ring around the airstrip, out to the distance you set: green on the map. It stays off the runway and 35 m either side of it, the ramp, taxiways, exclusion zones, objects and tie-downs, and off OpenStreetMap's roads, buildings and town areas and the sea (from the same terrain data as the visual approaches; lakes above sea level aren't detected). The type is suggested from AFAC's state and elevation: Baja desert, Northern desert foothills, Tropical dry scrub, Humid tropics or Highlands; change it if you know the area. Only X-Plane's own library is used, as the Scenery Gateway requires. X-Plane draws its cactus mix only inside its own cactus region, so these types have agave, yucca and scrub but no cactus.

                Exclusion zones go in the DSF as WorldEditor writes them (sim/exclude_… rectangles). "Import from Gateway" and "Open a scenery zip" bring a pack's zones in, so a pack you build under the Gateway's ID keeps the Gateway airport's tree and building exclusions instead of dropping them.

                To carry on editing somewhere else: open the downloaded zip there with "Open a scenery zip" (the zip carries the layout), or send "Share layout link" by text, email or AirDrop. Whoever opens the link gets this airfield with your runway ends, ramp, tie-downs, windsock and objects, ready to edit. Zips from WorldEditor or other X-Plane packs open too: the runway, ramp, windsock, start positions and any of this app's library objects come in; other objects are listed and left out.

                Each object appears on the ramp in the map above as an amber square with its rough footprint. Drag the square to move it, and set its heading (degrees true) in the list. Footprints are approximate; X-Plane draws each model at its own size. Which way a model's door faces depends on the model, so check it in X-Plane or WorldEditor.

                Data and thanks

                • AFAC (Agencia Federal de Aviación Civil), "Aeródromos y helipuertos" list on gob.mx: edition loading…
                • ICAO check: Mexico's official airport list, "Sistema Aeroportuario Mexicano" (gob.mx, 14-Mar-2025 edition), with positions and local codes from OurAirports (public domain). Both bundled into the app 14-Nov-2026.
                • OpenStreetMap: runway, ramp, taxiway and hangar shapes, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Read through the Overpass API. If an airstrip is missing there, adding it to OpenStreetMap helps every pilot who uses this app.
                • Magnetic variation: NOAA/NGA World Magnetic Model WMM-2025, computed in the page with the geomagnetism library (Apache 2.0).
                • With thanks to airports.robrc.net, the public directory of Mexican airport infrastructure that showed the way to AFAC's data. Its map and its corrections to AFAC's figures are worth a look.

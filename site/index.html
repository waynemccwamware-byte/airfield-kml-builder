Airfield KML Builder

v0.17

Purpose: Find a Mexican airfield in AFAC's official list, check that it isn't already an ICAO airport, and build a KML of its runway, ramp and taxiways from AFAC and OpenStreetMap data, to import into ForeFlight as a map layer.

1

Find the airfield

Search AFAC's list of aerodromes by designator (e.g. SBY), name or municipality. It's the same official list that airports.robrc.net puts on a searchable map of Mexico. That site showed us the way to this data, and it's well worth a visit.

    Is it already an ICAO airport?

    An airport with an ICAO code (MMxx) is normally in ForeFlight and Garmin Pilot already, runways and all. This checks AFAC's airfield against Mexico's official airport list (Sistema Aeroportuario Mexicano) and the ICAO codes in OurAirports: same 3-letter local code, or an ICAO airport within 3 km (1.6 NM) of AFAC's point.

      4

      Runway, ramp and taxiways

      Shapes come from OpenStreetMap, checked against AFAC's figures. AFAC gives only one reference point, the runway numbers, length, width and surface, so without OpenStreetMap the runway's position and direction would be guesses.

        Enter the runway ends yourself

        Use this when OpenStreetMap has no runway, or its runway looks wrong on the satellite view. In Google Maps or Google Earth, long-press each end of the runway and copy the coordinates (decimal degrees, e.g. 26.25824, -112.50316). Coordinates entered here replace OpenStreetMap's runway.

        5

        Preview and send to ForeFlight

        This is what the KML will draw. Zoom and pan, and switch between Satellite, Map and Topo (top right) to check the shapes against the ground before sending. Colors follow the runway surface: asphalt black, concrete gray, dirt brown, gravel tan, grass green. The ramp and taxiways are a shade lighter, and the airfield box lighter still.

        Use this when OpenStreetMap has no runway for this strip, or when its runway doesn't line up with the strip on the satellite view. Zoom in, tap the button, then tap each end of the runway. Your line replaces OpenStreetMap's runway; any ramp and taxiways stay. To undo it, use "Back to OpenStreetMap" in step 4. If the drawn runway already sits on the strip, you don't need this.

        Where each piece comes from

          On iPhone or iPad, tap "Open in ForeFlight": the KML opens as a file from a web link. Tap the share button and choose ForeFlight (it may be under More), and ForeFlight imports it as a User Map Layer. "Share KML…" opens the share sheet directly, but iOS doesn't always list ForeFlight there. On a computer, download the file and AirDrop or email it to the iPad.

          Data and thanks

          • AFAC (Agencia Federal de Aviación Civil), "Aeródromos y helipuertos" list on gob.mx: edition loading…
          • ICAO check: Mexico's official airport list, "Sistema Aeroportuario Mexicano" (gob.mx, 14-Mar-2025 edition), with positions and local codes from OurAirports (public domain). Both bundled into the app 14-Nov-2026.
          • OpenStreetMap: runway, ramp, taxiway and hangar shapes, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Read through the Overpass API. If a strip is missing there, adding it to OpenStreetMap helps every pilot who uses this app.
          • With thanks to airports.robrc.net, the public directory of Mexican airport infrastructure that showed the way to AFAC's data. Its map and its corrections to AFAC's figures are worth a look.

          Not for navigation. A planning and situational-awareness aid only. Positions come from published figures and volunteer mapping and can be wrong. Check every airfield against current official sources, recent pilot reports and your own eyes before using it.
